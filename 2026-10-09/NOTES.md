## 1. Who am I

- I'm an AI enthusiast, and an optimist
  - that's why I'm standing here!
  - I love Claude Code, use it since it's inception and started spreading the word.

## 2. The situation after a rollout

- one can face challenges to bring AI tools beyond "a replacement for Google" among your colleagues.
- people just don't think about what they could ask Claude
- there are always people that are more tool-curious
- we feel the freedom of experimentation, that's why we, this group of 4 power users, accelerate
  - others don't feel the freedom, or do not _get_ the freedom
  - as they work according to expectations, working through their tasks
- most people have structure, projects, do automated things, says Nuno
  - we need to convince the people to give them time to experiment


## 3. Who might be the hidden AI vanguard?

- they show a combination of the following
  - run out of tokens on a standard seat
  - they complain about this, and by talking about how or under which conditions they run into these issues, I come to understand how they apply AI tools.
    - heavy use is not necessarily good use
      - we had a coworker who tried to translate an Excel sheet of 10,000 rows and 8 columns be translated into 6 languages in one go in a web chat session. That didn't work out.
        - the intention was good, the understanding that AI should be capable of doing this was right, but the tool use was wrong...
        - we challenged the colleague to ask Claude for ways to make this process more token-efficient.
        - now they use Cowork with a set of prompts for this
      - we had a colleague who wanted to build an operational planning tool for multiple physical stores into an excelsheet.
        - this sounds like a good idea
        - but it almost ended up being a full fledged Saas application built into excel.
        - again: right thinking, wrong tool use.
        - we suggested to slim down the business case
        - and don't in it through regular chat, but rather through Cowork.
      - others found out themselves that a certain workflow was inefficient and researched for solutions by themselves.
        - how did we know? since we do openly discuss in a cross-department chat group.
  - they ask for connectors: how can I reach this or that data?
  - they ask about how can they enable tool x for more colleagues?
  - they come into meetings with pretty good slide decks suddenly
  - they move beyond their role
    - they start coding
    - they deliver mockups with proper company style guide applied
    - they deliver reports with new insights on existing data
- Monitor usage
  - Filter out the ones who embrace it best
  - Use their knowledge and especially their attitude for knowledge sharing


## 4. How to foster them?

- What do we do with the vanguards?
  - feed their curiosty with new tools
  - we discuss their current workflows
  - discuss their wishes: I wish I could X and then Y and even Z
    - usually they have a vision
  - we share about the ways we think in applying AI
- Start with standard seats first only then consider to move people to premium
- be willing to assign them an upgrade to their license seat so they can extend their exploration beyond the basics
  - This way they can not only speed up but also explore more
- Allow for different vendors it's pretty good to combine and ChatGPT alongside claude
  - Their desktop apps are of similar quality and capabilities
- Allow for licenses assigned to interns and trainees too
  - They're the new generation! Learn from them!
  - they might have used AI quite much already
- Allow people to experiment within their license seat
- Give them room to explore and experiment


## 5. Put the vanguards to work: spread adoption

- find the accelerators and assign them an additional role, for example to pass by other departments and discuss options of implementing AI, laat hen aanjagers zijn, de uitdagers. Zoek mensen die creatief zijn die durven te experimenteren en die ook goed kunnen vertellen. Vind de techneut die in goeie algemene bewoording kan uitleggen waarom efficiënt token gebruiken belangrijk is en hoe dat zou kunnen worden waargemaakt.
  - Vincent said: a head of tech staff of 100 hired 3 to 4 people just to walk around the work space and nudge, support and explain how tasks could be automated with AI.
- people feel overwhelmed in bigger group settings
  - better for smaller groups, Nuno suggests to have meetings with managers and showcase them so they can spread within their team.
  - convince managers to tell where to improve your team
  - is plenary the right way? Reynout asks; probably not
    - should there be another way to share?
    - smaller groups is better, focusing on one person's tasks
- When in a meeting and a discussion point comes up, check it with Claude immediately
  - "let's review after the meeting" -> No! Do it directly
  - this is a way to have employees learn
  - a live demo on your own company's data
- we should serve the food on the plate instead of in the mouth
- share skill of info / newsletter routine for daily message on your domain
  - could be recent court cases around accessibility, maybe one mail every week
  - CVE vulnerabilities once a week: do wee need to apply a hot fix for our software
- reporting: do not be afraid for the scary 'technical' challenges, just use your natural language
  - instruct claude as if it were your coworker
  - don't ask me the question, ask Claude just the question
  - tangible use cases to share to spread the curiosity
- Challenge your employees
  - Ask them on each Friday which task have you been doing last week that could have been automated by AI
- FrAIday chat group
- on every Friday, each worker in the office
  - "what task did I do this week that I could have done with AI"
  - our quest is to show
  - employee does a lot of manual tasks, hates to do a lot of manual tasks
    - engage them with AI to see if they can apply AI in their work
  - Vincent says: but people maybe cannot imagine what task could be automated by AI.


## 6. Guardrails: the vanguards know the limits

- our quest is to show where the limits and risks are
- Also, maybe we know better because we have some sense of AI's limitations, capabilities and we know the risks of applying AI on certain systems/data.
- BigQuery and Claude
  - some colleagues ask: can you give me access to BigQuery?
  - at first sight, this seems to be "The Solution", but it's not
  - real business cases is what we should know, since
    - information is often already available in a different source
    - it can become very expensive to just query BQ
    - just access to everything isn't always the solution
- enable playing fields, Reynout says
  - somehow make them aware of the consequences of heavy queries on the BigQuery for example


## 7. Takeaway

- A few weeks after a Claude/ChatGPT rollout, identify the vanguards, foster them, and use their experience for further enhancements.
