The Hidden AI Vanguards

Stay on the lookout for that accelerating coworker, and foster them!

Vincent Bruijn

1 · who am I

An AI optimist

  • That is why I am standing here
  • Using Claude Code since its first release
  • Spreading the word ever since
  • One of the AI go-to people at G-STAR, together with our CTO

A view on adoption

  • Our CEO saw the opportunity: licenses for Claude and ChatGPT
  • I regularly align with colleagues on how and why they use AI
  • I give advice and suggestions, and share knowledge

2 · after the rollout

A replacement for Google...?

  • Hard to get AI tools beyond search
  • People just don't think about what they could ask Claude
  • But some people are always more tool-curious

Freedom to experiment

  • A few power users accelerate: they feel free to experiment
  • Others don't feel that freedom, or don't get it
  • They work to expectations, task by task
  • Most have structure, projects and routines

Convince people to give them time to experiment

3 · spot them

Who might be the hidden AI vanguard?

A combination of these signs

  • They run out of tokens on a standard seat
  • They ask for connectors: "how can I reach this data?"
  • They ask how to enable a tool for more colleagues
  • They suddenly bring good slide decks to meetings
  • They move beyond their role

Heavy use is not good use

They complain about limits. Ask how they hit them.

case · translation A sheet of 10,000 rows by 8 columns, into 6 languages, in one web chat. Right idea, wrong tool. We asked them to ask Claude for a token-efficient way. Now: Cowork with a set of prompts.
case · store planning An operational planning tool for multiple stores, built in Excel. It almost became a full SaaS app inside a spreadsheet. We slimmed down the business case and moved it to Cowork.
Image 1: Cowork with the translation prompts

Beyond their role

  • They start coding
  • They deliver mockups in the company style guide
  • They deliver reports with new insights on existing data
  • They spot inefficient workflows and find solutions themselves

We see it because we discuss openly in a cross-department chat group

Image 2: a mockup made by a non-designer

Monitor usage

  • Find the ones who embrace it best
  • Use their knowledge
  • And especially their attitude, for knowledge sharing
Image 3: anonymized usage dashboard

4 · foster them

What do we do with the vanguards?

  • Feed their curiosity with new tools
  • Discuss their current workflows
  • Discuss their wishes: "I wish I could X, then Y, and even Z"
  • Share how we think about applying AI

They usually have a vision

Licenses

  • Start with standard seats, only then consider premium
  • Be willing to upgrade a vanguard's seat
  • So they not only speed up, but also explore more
  • Let people experiment within their seat

Room to explore

  • Allow different vendors: ChatGPT alongside Claude
  • Their desktop apps are of similar quality
  • Give interns and trainees licenses too
  • They are the new generation, and may use AI a lot already

Learn from them

5 · spread adoption

Put the vanguards to work

  • Give them an extra role: visit other departments
  • Let them be the drivers and the challengers
  • Creative people who dare to experiment, and tell it well
  • The techie who explains in plain words why token efficiency matters

A head of tech with 100 staff hired 3 to 4 people just to walk around, nudge and explain what AI could automate

Small groups over plenary

  • People feel overwhelmed in big group sessions
  • Showcase to managers, so they spread it within their team
  • Let managers point out where their team can improve
  • Focus on one person's tasks

Serve the food on the plate, not in the mouth

Don't ask me, ask Claude

  • A question comes up in a meeting? Check it with Claude, right now
  • Not "let's review after the meeting"
  • A live demo on your own company data
  • No fear of "technical": instruct Claude like a coworker
  • Share routines: a weekly mail on accessibility court cases, a weekly CVE check

FrAIday

What task did I do this week that I could have done with AI?
  • Every Friday, everyone in the office, in a shared chat group
  • Start with people who hate their manual tasks
  • But people may not imagine what AI can automate: show them
Image 4: the FrAIday chat group (anonymized)

6 · guardrails

The vanguards know the limits

  • Our job is to show where the limits and risks are
  • We have a sense of what AI can and can't do
  • And of the risks of applying it to certain systems and data

"Can I get access to BigQuery?"

  • At first sight: the solution. It is not.
  • We need the real business case
  • The information is often already available elsewhere
  • Querying it freely can become very expensive
  • Access to everything is not always the answer
Image 5: query cost of a heavy BigQuery request

Enable playing fields

  • Safe spaces to experiment
  • Make people aware of the consequences of heavy queries

7 · takeaway

A few weeks after a rollout

  1. Identify the vanguards
  2. Foster them
  3. Use their experience for the next step
